# Fungicide Application Practice Note

## Coverage and water volume

Most spray failures are coverage failures, not product failures. A foliar fungicide only protects the tissue it physically reaches, so the target is run-off-point wetting of both leaf surfaces including the underside, where the majority of spores germinate. For a staked tomato or potato crop at full canopy, plan on 400 to 500 litres of spray solution per acre; a young crop with an open canopy needs 200 to 250 litres. Mixing at the correct concentration and then applying too little volume is the most common way a correctly chosen product fails.

## Timing

Spray in the early morning or late evening so the leaf surface stays wet long enough for the product to bind. Application in the middle of a hot day dries the droplet before the active ingredient is taken up and can scorch treated foliage. Do not spray if rain is expected within four hours, because an unbound protectant washes off and the application is wasted; if rain falls within that window the application should be repeated once the canopy has dried. Wind above roughly 10 kilometres per hour causes drift and uneven coverage and is a reason to postpone.

## Equipment and calibration

Calibrate the sprayer before the first application of the season by spraying water over a measured area and checking the volume used against the intended rate. Use a hollow-cone nozzle for foliar disease work and replace nozzles that show a widened or distorted pattern, since a worn nozzle silently increases delivered volume and effective dose. Agitate the tank during application, as wettable powders such as mancozeb settle out within minutes of standing and the last rows then receive a concentration far above the intended one.

## Operator safety and intervals

Wear gloves, a mask and full-sleeved clothing while mixing and spraying, and mix in the open rather than in an enclosed store. Do not re-enter a treated block until the foliage is dry, and observe the pre-harvest interval printed on the product label before picking from treated rows. Empty containers should be triple-rinsed and punctured, never reused for water or food storage.
