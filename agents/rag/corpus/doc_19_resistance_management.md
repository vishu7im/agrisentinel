# Fungicide Resistance Management Guidelines

## Why rotation matters

Repeated use of a single mode of action drives resistance. A fungal population carries rare individuals insensitive to a given chemistry; every application of that chemistry removes their competition, and within a few seasons the insensitive individuals are the population. The visible symptom is a product that worked last year and does nothing this year, at the same dose, against the same disease. Rotation of modes of action is the only practical defence available to a grower, and it costs nothing to plan.

## How to rotate

Alternate between chemical groups rather than between brand names, because several products sold under different names share one active ingredient and rotating among them is not a rotation at all. Multi-site protectants such as mancozeb and chlorothalonil are at low risk of resistance and can anchor a programme. Single-site systemics — the strobilurins such as azoxystrobin and pyraclostrobin, and the triazoles such as difenoconazole, propiconazole and tebuconazole — are at higher risk and should be limited to two applications per season each, never applied consecutively.

## Dose and interval discipline

Do not reduce the label dose to stretch a product. A sub-lethal dose is the ideal condition for selecting resistant individuals, since it kills the sensitive population without controlling the insensitive one. Equally, do not shorten the interval between applications below the label minimum in the hope of faster control; excess frequency increases selection pressure without improving the outcome. Where a systemic is used, pairing it with a multi-site protectant in the same application reduces selection pressure on the systemic component.

## Signs a programme is failing

Treat a lack of response as a diagnostic result, not a reason to increase the dose. If disease continues to advance seven days after a correctly applied and correctly timed application, the likely explanations are, in order: incomplete spray coverage, a misidentified disease, and resistance. Check the first two before concluding the third, and change the mode of action rather than the rate.
