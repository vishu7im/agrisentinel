# Potato Late Blight and Early Blight — Field Management

## Potato late blight — identification

Potato late blight is caused by the same pathogen as tomato late blight, Phytophthora infestans, and the two crops infect each other, so a nearby infected tomato plot is a live source for a potato crop and the reverse. Lesions begin as water-soaked dark green patches at leaf tips and margins, expand rapidly, and develop a white sporulating ring on the underside in humid conditions. A field can go from first lesion to complete haulm destruction in seven to ten days under cool wet weather, which is why this disease is treated as an emergency and others are not.

## Potato late blight — tuber protection

The yield loss that matters is in the tubers, not the foliage. Spores wash from infected leaves into the soil and infect tubers through lenticels and wounds, producing a firm brown rot that continues to develop in storage and can destroy a stored crop weeks after harvest. Earthing up to give a deeper soil covering over developing tubers is a direct and effective protection. Where infection is severe, cutting and removing the haulm two to three weeks before harvest deprives the tubers of an inoculum source and is often worth more than an additional spray.

## Potato late blight — chemical control

Apply mancozeb 75 percent WP at 2.0 to 2.5 grams per litre of water as a protectant on first detection or ahead of forecast wet weather, and repeat at 7 day intervals while conditions remain favourable. Where infection is established or the weather is continuing wet, use a systemic combination — cymoxanil 8 percent plus mancozeb 64 percent WP at 3.0 grams per litre, or metalaxyl 8 percent plus mancozeb 64 percent WP at 2.5 grams per litre — limited to two applications per season, alternated with a protectant.

## Potato early blight — identification and control

Potato early blight, caused by Alternaria solani, shows the same concentric-ringed dark lesions as on tomato, beginning on the oldest lower leaves and progressing upward. It is a slower disease than late blight and rarely warrants the same urgency. Mancozeb 75 percent WP at 2.0 to 2.5 grams per litre at first symptom, repeated at 10 day intervals, is normally sufficient; azoxystrobin 23 percent SC at 1.0 millilitre per litre is available where defoliation is advanced. Adequate nitrogen and potassium nutrition measurably reduces susceptibility.

## Follow-up and seed for next season

Re-inspect seven days after any application, and for late blight inspect after four days if wet weather has continued, since the disease can outrun a weekly cycle. Do not save seed tubers from an infected field. Cull and destroy volunteer potatoes and cull piles before the next season, as these are the most common route by which late blight survives the interval between crops.
