# Corn Foliar Diseases — Common Rust, Northern Leaf Blight and Gray Leaf Spot

## Common rust — identification

Common rust of maize, caused by Puccinia sorghi, produces small oval to elongated pustules scattered on both upper and lower leaf surfaces. Fresh pustules rupture the leaf surface and release a cinnamon-brown powder that rubs off onto a finger, which is the quickest field confirmation available. Pustules appear on both surfaces of the leaf, unlike southern rust which is largely confined to the upper surface. The pathogen does not overwinter in most temperate maize areas; it arrives on wind currents, so an outbreak is not evidence of a mistake made in the previous season.

## Northern leaf blight — identification

Northern leaf blight, caused by Exserohilum turcicum, produces long cigar-shaped grey-green to tan lesions, typically 3 to 15 centimetres in length, running parallel with the leaf veins. Lesions begin on lower leaves and progress upward. The damage that matters is loss of green leaf area above the ear during grain fill, and infection that reaches the ear leaf before silking can cost a substantial share of yield. Cool, moist conditions between 18 and 27 degrees Celsius with prolonged dew favour the disease.

## Gray leaf spot — identification

Gray leaf spot, caused by Cercospora zeae-maydis, produces narrow rectangular lesions sharply bounded by the leaf veins, which gives them a distinctive blocky appearance quite unlike the tapered ends of northern leaf blight. Lesions are tan to grey and may join to blight an entire leaf. The pathogen survives on maize residue, so continuous maize under reduced or no tillage carries the highest risk, and the disease is favoured by warm humid weather with extended dew periods.

## Chemical control for all three

The decision to spray maize is economic and depends on growth stage: a foliar fungicide is generally justified when disease reaches the ear leaf before or around tasselling on a susceptible hybrid, and is rarely justified once grain fill is well advanced. Propiconazole 25 percent EC at 1.0 millilitre per litre of water, tebuconazole 25.9 percent EC at 1.0 millilitre per litre, or pyraclostrobin 20 percent WG at 1.0 gram per litre are the standard options, applied once at tasselling with a second application 14 to 21 days later only if disease continues to advance. Mancozeb 75 percent WP at 2.0 to 2.5 grams per litre is an option for rust in seed and nursery plots.

## Cultural control and prevention

Hybrid resistance is the primary control for all three diseases and is chosen at planting, not after symptoms appear. For northern leaf blight and gray leaf spot, which survive on residue, rotate out of maize for one season and incorporate residue by tillage where the field allows it. Common rust does not survive on residue, so rotation does not help against it and only hybrid selection and timely fungicide do. Avoid excessive plant density, which extends dew periods within the canopy.
