# Tomato Late Blight - Field Identification and Management

## Identification

Late blight, caused by Phytophthora infestans, appears first as water-soaked grey-green lesions on older leaves, often at the leaf margin. Under cool, humid conditions the lesions expand rapidly and a white sporulating growth becomes visible on the underside. Because spores travel on wind and splash, infection typically advances as an expanding front rather than uniformly across a field. A grower who finds lesions on only one edge of a plot should assume the rest of the plot is already exposed and inspect downwind rows the same day.

## Conditions that drive an outbreak

The pathogen needs leaf wetness and moderate temperature. Night temperatures between 10 and 20 degrees Celsius with relative humidity above 90 percent, or two consecutive overcast days with rain, are enough to take a field from a few lesions to visible collapse within a week. Dense canopies, overhead irrigation and low-lying waterlogged patches all extend the hours of leaf wetness and should be treated as higher risk during scouting.

## First response when infection is confirmed

Act within 48 hours of confirming late blight. Remove and destroy visibly infected foliage and any fallen fruit from the affected zone before spraying, because treated surfaces are recontaminated by sporulating tissue left in place. Stop overhead irrigation in the affected block and switch to furrow or drip watering where the layout allows. If more than a quarter of the field is affected and wet weather is forecast to continue, prioritise protecting the unaffected area over treating the worst rows.

## Chemical control

Where infection is limited to identifiable clusters, a protectant is sufficient: mancozeb 75 percent WP at 2.0 to 2.5 grams per litre of water, or chlorothalonil 75 percent WP at 2.0 grams per litre, applied to the cluster and a buffer of at least two rows. Where infection exceeds roughly 25 percent of the scanned area or wet weather is continuing, a systemic combination such as cymoxanil 8 percent plus mancozeb 64 percent WP at 3.0 grams per litre gives curative action on tissue already infected. Do not apply a systemic product more than twice in one season.

## Follow-up and re-inspection

Re-inspect the field seven days after the first application. Compare the proportion of affected plants against the original assessment: a stable or falling proportion means the protectant is holding and the same product may be repeated, while a rising proportion means the treatment is not keeping pace and a systemic product with a different mode of action is warranted. Harvest intervals must be respected, and fruit from treated rows should not be picked before the interval printed on the product label has elapsed.
