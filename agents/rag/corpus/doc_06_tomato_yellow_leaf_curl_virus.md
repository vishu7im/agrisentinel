# Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus and Whitefly Vector Management

## Identification

Tomato yellow leaf curl virus produces upward cupping and curling of leaflets, marked yellowing of leaf margins, a reduction in leaf size, and severe stunting with shortened internodes that gives infected plants a bushy appearance. Flowers drop and fruit set collapses; a plant infected early may set no marketable fruit at all. Symptoms appear two to three weeks after the plant is fed on by a viruliferous whitefly, so the distribution of symptoms today reflects where the vector was a fortnight ago.

## There is no cure for an infected plant

This is a virus, and no fungicide, bactericide or foliar nutrient will cure an infected plant. Any recommendation to spray an infected plant back to health is wrong and should be refused. The entire management programme is therefore directed at two things: removing infected plants as sources of further virus, and controlling the whitefly vector Bemisia tabaci that moves it. A plan that treats this disease like a fungal infection will cost the grower money and the field its crop.

## Roguing infected plants

Remove symptomatic plants promptly and completely, including the root, and carry them out of the field in a closed bag rather than dragging them between rows, since disturbed whiteflies fly to neighbouring plants. Destroy them by burning or deep burial. Rogue in the early morning when whiteflies are least active. Where infection is scattered at low incidence, roguing plus vector control can save the crop; where it is widespread and the crop is young, replanting with resistant varieties is usually the better economic decision.

## Vector control

Control whitefly rather than the virus. Imidacloprid 17.8 percent SL at 0.3 millilitres per litre of water, or thiamethoxam 25 percent WG at 0.3 grams per litre, applied to the underside of foliage where the nymphs sit, are the standard options; rotate between them and do not use the same insecticide group for more than two consecutive applications. Yellow sticky traps at 10 to 12 per acre both monitor and reduce the adult population. Neem oil at 5 millilitres per litre is a lower-impact option for light populations and is compatible with natural enemies.

## Prevention for the following season

Raise seedlings under 40-mesh insect-proof netting, since most field infection traces back to seedlings already infected in the nursery. Use resistant or tolerant varieties where available, as this removes more risk than any spray programme. Remove solanaceous weeds and volunteer tomato plants from field margins, which host both the virus and the vector between crops, and avoid planting a new tomato crop adjacent to an old one that is being cleared.
