# Tomato Early Blight — Identification and Management

## Identification

Early blight is caused by Alternaria solani and is recognised by dark brown to black lesions carrying concentric rings, giving a target-board appearance, usually surrounded by a yellow halo. Unlike late blight it begins on the oldest, lowest leaves and works upward, and the lesions are dry and firm rather than water-soaked. Severe infection causes progressive defoliation from the base of the plant, which exposes fruit to sunscald and reduces yield even where the fruit itself is never infected.

## Conditions that drive an outbreak

The pathogen favours warm temperatures between 24 and 29 degrees Celsius with alternating wet and dry periods, so it is often more damaging in a season of intermittent showers than in continuous rain. It survives on crop debris and on solanaceous weeds between seasons. Plants under stress from nitrogen deficiency or heavy fruit load are markedly more susceptible, which is why a nutrition check belongs in the response alongside any spray decision.

## First response when infection is confirmed

Remove and destroy the lowest affected leaves, which both removes inoculum and improves air movement at the base of the canopy. Confirm that the crop is not nitrogen deficient, since correcting nutrition slows the disease measurably in a way no fungicide substitutes for. Mulching reduces rain splash from soil-borne debris onto lower foliage and is worth doing before the first application rather than after.

## Chemical control

Mancozeb 75 percent WP at 2.0 to 2.5 grams per litre of water is the standard protectant and is applied at the first appearance of lesions, repeated at 7 to 10 day intervals while conditions remain favourable. Where defoliation is already advanced, difenoconazole 25 percent EC at 0.5 millilitres per litre or azoxystrobin 23 percent SC at 1.0 millilitre per litre provides systemic control, limited to two applications per season each. Chlorothalonil 75 percent WP at 2.0 grams per litre is an alternative protectant for rotation.

## Follow-up

Re-inspect after 7 days and record whether new lesions have appeared above the treated tier of foliage. Upward progression despite treatment usually indicates that coverage did not reach the leaf underside rather than that the product failed. Early blight rarely justifies more than three or four applications in a season; if the crop is approaching harvest and the upper canopy is intact, further spraying adds cost without protecting yield.
